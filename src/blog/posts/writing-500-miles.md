Act One of *500 Miles, 2 Hearts and a Guitar* ends with two blunt words: "Go home."

Randy says them to Jordan after he drives five hundred miles to surprise her in person, at the exact moment her father is dying in the next room. It's not really about Jordan. But he doesn't know that yet, and neither does the audience — not for a while.

That's the gap the song "500 Miles" exists to fill.

It's Jordan's solo in the second act, and it's the first time we hear what's actually going on inside him after that rejection — not the deflecting, joke-cracking Jordan we've come to know, but the real one. The song moves through three movements: anger, then ambivalence, then collapse. He starts sure he's the wronged party. By the end he's not sure of anything.

While writing the lyrics I noticed "miles" can be sung with one syllable or two. In the chorus:

> *Five hundred miles with your song playing on repeat*\
> *Five hundred miles and the door was closed and left us incomplete*

I caught myself singing the first "miles" differently than the second, depending on the melody. It felt important to settle on one way and stay consistent.

The staging idea I keep coming back to: Jordan holds the guitar through the whole song — his instrument, his connection to Randy's late father, the thing that's tied every good moment in this show together — and loses his grip on it as the song goes on. By the final chord, he's set it down. That chord is unresolved. It just hangs there.

I like that it doesn't resolve. Neither does he — it's the start of his downward spiral.
